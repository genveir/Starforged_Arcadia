# Arcadia: Tasks

Tasks are a small system bolted onto the side of Starforged. They cover the quiet work of a life in space: little jobs, favours, learning a craft, settling in, and making the ship a home. They do not replace any Starforged move and are not integrated with Starforged's economy. Momentum, legacy tracks, experience and assets work exactly as written and are never earned through tasks.

## Task or not

**Tasks are never relevant under threat.** If something is threatening the character, the stretch is played with the regular moves (Face Danger, Secure an Advantage, and so on) and the task waits.

**Would failure be interesting?** If failing would make a story (someone is stranded or hurt, a relationship sours, a rival gets there first), it is a vow, a move or a scene, played with the full Starforged machinery. If failing only means the work takes longer and wears you down, it is a task.

The same project can fall on either side. Installing a galley from a manual over a quiet week is a task. Installing one with parts bought from a fixer who wants a favour in return is a vow with Compel and Check Your Gear along the way. The GM calls which side a project falls on, says so, and defers to the player.

## Satisfaction

Satisfaction is a track from 0 with no upper limit. It is a buffer and nothing more.

- **Gaining it.** Through Reap the Benefits, and through Find Your Rhythm.
- **Spending it.** Only on the suffer move a task miss calls for. Instead of taking the -1 on a meter, the player may pay it as -1 satisfaction. No other cost in the game can be paid in satisfaction.
- **At 0.** Nothing happens. There is no impact. A task miss is simply paid from the regular meters.

## Progress boxes

A task has between 1 and 10 progress boxes, drawn when it is made. Boxes fill left to right and each holds four ticks. When marking more than one tick, carry over into the next box. If a tick would go into a box that was not drawn, draw it now. If the tenth box fills, ignore all leftover ticks.

A task does not need every box filled to be finished. Fewer filled boxes means a smaller reward.

If all drawn boxes fill and the task is still not done, either draw new boxes and keep going, or treat it as one stage of a larger job: Reap the Benefits and Make a Task for the next stage.

**No pressure.** A task should never be played for drama. Don't introduce stakes or twists for tension. If a task is canceled, that's unfortunate, but no one will hold it against the character.

## MAKE A TASK

**When you commit to a job, take on someone's small request, start learning a skill, or set out to improve your ship or your life aboard it**, and it passes the test above, consider the urgency and complexity of the task and draw progress boxes using the table below.

|  | Simple | Complicated | Puzzling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Laid-Back** | 1 progress box | 2 progress boxes | 3 progress boxes |
| **Urgent** | 2 progress boxes | 3 progress boxes | 6 progress boxes |
| **Critical** | 3 progress boxes | 6 progress boxes | 10 progress boxes |

Give the task a name and envision the steps it might take. Then *Work on a Task*.

Notes. Urgency: a closing opportunity or a social deadline means critical, such as a dock berth that is open for two days or a wedding tomorrow. Danger never sets urgency, because a task under threat is not a task. All the time in the world means laid-back. Anything between is urgent.

Complexity: trained, experienced and properly equipped means simple. No idea where to begin means puzzling. Anything between is complicated. A relevant skill is the usual reason a task is simple.

When genuinely unsure, urgent and complicated gives 3 boxes and is always a safe default.

## WORK ON A TASK

**When you work towards finishing a task**, envision your action and roll +stat. Add +1 if one of your skills applies. Add a connection's +1 if they aid you on a task matching their role.

On a **strong hit**, mark 2 ticks on the task.

On a **weak hit**, mark 1 tick on the task, then suffer -1 satisfaction, this can not roll over into other meters, nothing bad happens if you're already at 0.

On a **miss**, you make no progress. Envision what went wrong, then suffer -1 on a meter of your choice (health, spirit, supply or momentum, whichever the fiction supports). You may pay this as -1 satisfaction instead.

Notes. A task roll is outside Starforged's momentum economy. You cannot burn momentum on it, negative momentum does not cancel the action die, a "+1 on your next action die roll" from Secure an Advantage or Gain Ground neither applies to it nor is used up by it, and a hit gains no momentum. Momentum only moves on a task when a miss is paid there.

A miss on a task never escalates. There is no Pay the Price and no new threat. Matches colour the fiction (a happy accident, a memorably stubborn bolt) but change nothing mechanically.

Not every bit of work calls for a roll. Do not roll for the dull parts; assume they happen in the background.

---

## REAP THE BENEFITS

**When you finish a task, or bring a stage of one to a close**, envision the result. The thing you worked on happens in the fiction whatever you choose below. Then pick one:

- **If finishing this task fills you with satisfaction,** gain 2 satisfaction per filled box.
- **If finishing this task has made you better at a skill,** learn that skill or take its upgrade, and gain 1 satisfaction per filled box.

You can only reap a task with at least one filled box.

Notes. The reward is the player's choice and does not have to match the obvious source. The galley gets installed whichever is picked.

---

## FIND YOUR RHYTHM

**When you set aside time for something you enjoy for its own sake**, such as tinkering with something that is not a task, cooking a proper meal, playing music, or watching a star set from the observation port, roll +stat.

On a **strong hit**, gain 3 satisfaction.

On a **weak hit**, gain 2 satisfaction, but the moment is interrupted. Envision how. -1 momentum.

On a **miss**, you find no comfort. Pay the Price. This cost cannot be paid in satisfaction.

This move cannot raise satisfaction above 5. Like tasks, it is not available under threat.

Notes. This is the way back for a character whose buffer has run dry, not a way to build one. It is separate from Hearten, which restores spirit and is about other people.

## SUB-TASKS

**When you make a task that serves a larger task**, judge how directly it helps and set how much of its progress carries over into the larger one:

| How it helps the main task | Carryover |
|---|---|
| Does nothing for it | None |
| Helps a little | 1 tick per filled box |
| Makes it much easier | 2 ticks per filled box |
| Is not the main task, but leads directly to its success | 3 ticks per filled box |
| Is a hard requirement of it | Track progress on both tasks in full |

Mark the carryover on the main task when a box on the sub-task fills. The sub-task is still reaped normally. One sub-task can feed more than one main task, at its own rate for each.

Carryover is transitive: if carried ticks fill a box on a task that itself carries over into another task, that task gets its carryover too. Nothing carries over into a task that has already been finished, and nothing carries over into a vow.

Example: *Fit out the galley* carries 2 ticks per box into *Learn shipboard cooking* and 3 into *Make the ship a home*.

---

## CANCEL A TASK

**When you drop a task for any reason**, envision explaining it to whoever is most affected, even if that is only yourself, and envision their response. Gain 1 satisfaction per filled box.

Plans change. Dropping a task costs nothing in anyone's regard.

## Skills

Skills are custom and defined in play. There is no list. A skill is whatever fits the setting and the character: *Galley Cooking*, *Hydroponics*, *Lightwave Reconstruction*. The player chooses the character's starting skills.

**Learning.** A skill is learned or improved through Reap the Benefits on a task that genuinely exercised it, one step per task. The usual way is to say so up front: "I'm starting a task to learn Lightwave Reconstruction." A task that had the character doing the work of a skill they do not have can teach its basics. To improve a skill they already have, the task should have them do something they were not capable of before.

| Tier | Effect |
|---|---|
| Learn the basics | +1 on task rolls where this skill helps. |
| Upgrade | On a weak or strong hit, mark one additional tick on the task being worked on. |

A skill adds +1 whenever it genuinely helps the task, even outside its obvious home. Its upgrade only applies when that skill is the one in play for the roll.

Skills apply to task rolls only. They give nothing on Starforged moves; that is what assets are for.
