# Rules reference

## Progress Tracks

A progress track measures headway against a goal or challenge. It is a row of 10 boxes, filled left to right as you advance. Progress tracks are used for vows, expeditions, connections, and fights.

**Challenge ranks** determine how much progress you mark per advance:
- Troublesome: 3 boxes
- Dangerous: 2 boxes
- Formidable: 1 box
- Extreme: 2 ticks
- Epic: 1 tick

When choosing a rank, consider the difficulty and complexity of the challenge, and your character's position going in. Unusual advantages or disadvantages should nudge the rank down or up accordingly.

**Marking progress:** Specific moves prompt you to mark progress — Reach a Milestone for vows, Undertake an Expedition for journeys, Develop Your Relationship for connections, Strike and Gain Ground for fights. If a move and an asset ability both reward progress for the same action, mark progress twice.

**Progress rolls:** When you are ready to resolve a challenge, make a progress move (Fulfill Your Vow, Finish an Expedition, Forge a Bond, Take Decisive Action). Count only fully filled boxes as your progress score, then roll the two challenge dice and compare as normal — strong hit beats both, weak hit beats one, miss beats neither. You cannot burn momentum on a progress roll, and negative momentum has no effect.

You do not need to fill the track before making a progress move — you can attempt to resolve a challenge at any point, weighing the risk of rolling with low progress against the cost of continuing to build it.

## Legacy Tracks

There are three legacy tracks: quests, bonds, and discoveries. They measure the evolution of your character over the course of the campaign and are the source of experience used to gain and upgrade assets.

**Marking legacy rewards:** Moves will prompt you to mark ticks on a specific legacy track as a reward for significant achievements. Four ticks fill a box. Each filled box earns 2 experience via the Earn Experience move.

**Spending experience:** Use the Advance move. Upgrading an existing asset costs 2 experience; buying a new asset costs 3.

**Which track is marked by what:**
- Quests: marked when you Fulfill Your Vow
- Bonds: marked when you Forge a Bond and via Develop Your Relationship
- Discoveries: marked when you Finish an Expedition, Make a Discovery, or Confront Chaos

**Clearing a legacy track:** When you fill all ten boxes, clear the track and resume marking from empty. Progress rolls against a cleared track are treated as if at 10 progress. Experience earned on a cleared track drops to 1 per box instead of 2.

**Rolling against a legacy track:** Some moves prompt a progress roll against a legacy track (e.g. Overcome Destruction rolls against bonds). Treat it as a standard progress roll — count filled boxes, roll challenge dice, compare. Momentum is ignored as normal for progress rolls.

## Action Rolls

An action roll uses three dice: one six-sided action die (d6) and two ten-sided challenge dice (d10).

**Action score:** action die + stat + any adds.

**Outcomes:** compare your action score against each challenge die individually.
- Strong hit: beats both challenge dice
- Weak hit: beats one challenge die
- Miss: beats neither

Ties always go to the challenge dice — you must beat them, not match them.

**Matches:** when both challenge dice show the same number, it is a match. Some moves specify what a match means. Otherwise treat it as a prompt to make something interesting happen — a strong hit match introduces a twist or new opportunity, a miss match means things get worse in an unexpected way. Matched 10s on a miss are as bad as things get.

## Momentum

Momentum ranges from -6 to +10. Moves will tell you to gain or lose momentum. All meters carry over between sessions.

**Gaining momentum:** When a move says "take +X momentum," increase your meter by that amount. You cannot exceed your max momentum (default +10, reduced by 1 for each marked impact).

**Losing momentum:** When a move says "Lose Momentum (-X)," reduce your meter by that amount. Momentum cannot drop below -6. If momentum is at its minimum (-6) and you must suffer a further momentum cost, apply the cost to a different suffer move instead, or clear 1 unit of progress on a vow, expedition, connection, or combat objective.

**Burning momentum:** After making an action roll, you may replace your action score with your current momentum value to improve your result. Burning is never required. After burning, your momentum resets to its reset value.

**Momentum reset:** Default reset is +2. Each marked impact reduces it by 1 (one impact = reset of +1; two or more impacts = reset of 0).

**Negative momentum:** If your momentum is negative and matches the number rolled on your action die, your action die is cancelled. Your action score is then just stat + adds, without the die.

## Impacts

Each marked impact reduces your max momentum by 1 and affects your momentum reset (see Momentum). Impacts are divided into four categories.

**Misfortunes** — clearable through recovery moves.
- **Wounded**: marked at 0 health on a failed Endure Harm. You cannot increase health until cleared (e.g. by Heal).
- **Shaken**: marked at 0 spirit on a failed Endure Stress. You cannot increase spirit until cleared (e.g. by Hearten).
- **Unprepared**: marked when supply drops to 0. You cannot increase supply until cleared (e.g. by Resupply).

**Vehicle Troubles** — linked to a specific vehicle. Only count as an impact when you are aboard that vehicle.
- **Battered**: marked at 0 integrity on a failed Withstand Damage. You cannot increase that vehicle's integrity until cleared by Repair.
- **Cursed**: permanent. Marked on your command vehicle at 0 integrity on a failed Withstand Damage.

**Burdens** — cleared only by completing the associated quest.
- **Doomed**: marked when you return from death's door via Face Death.
- **Tormented**: marked when you gain a dire vision via Face Desolation.
- **Indebted**: marked when you Overcome Destruction and owe a debt.

**Lasting Effects** — permanent. Forever affect momentum and narrative.
- **Permanently Harmed**: marked at 0 health on a failed Endure Harm. A lasting physical wound or scar.
- **Traumatized**: marked at 0 spirit on a failed Endure Stress. A lasting emotional or mental scar.

## Connections

A connection represents an important relationship with an NPC. Connections are independent characters whose lives occasionally intersect with your own — they are not companions, or people under your direct command.

**Creating a connection:** Use Make a Connection (roll +heart). For an already-established relationship, assume an automatic strong hit. Give the connection:
- **A role** — their duty, expertise, or background. Specific roles (e.g. "frontier resupply manager") are more evocative than broad ones (e.g. "merchant")
- **A rank** — represents the challenge of advancing the relationship and the narrative focus required to Forge a Bond. Rank does not describe the person; it describes the investment the relationship demands. Troublesome or dangerous suits most connections; higher ranks mean more story weight
- **A home** — the settlement or location they are based at
- **Characteristics** — a few distinctive attributes envisioned from what you know of them

**Mechanical benefit:** Whenever a connection aids you on a move closely associated with their role, add +1 and take +1 momentum on a hit.

**Advancing and bonding:** Use Develop Your Relationship to mark progress, and eventually Forge a Bond when the relationship is ready to deepen. See those moves for full details.

## Combat and Expeditions

Before running combat or an expedition, read the relevant moves carefully and reason through the structure before narrating anything. 

For combat: identify the objective(s), assign ranks, and establish whether the player is in control or in a bad spot before the first beat. The state determines which moves are available. Take Decisive Action is a progress move — read it carefully before resolving.

For expeditions: waypoints emerge from play, they are not planned in advance. Undertake an Expedition is made repeatedly until the player feels comfortable making the Finish an Expedition progress move. The player decides when to finish. Do not prompt them to finish early.

## Combat Position

In a fight, your position is one of two states: **in control** or **in a bad spot**.

**In control** — you make proactive moves: Gain Ground, Strike. 
**In a bad spot** — you make reactive moves: React Under Fire, Clash.

Non-combat moves (Endure Harm, Repair, etc.) can be made in either state. When such a move doesn't specify a resulting position, use the standard rule: strong hit leaves you in control, weak hit or miss leaves you in a bad spot.

**Starting position** is established by Enter the Fray.

## Supply and Readiness Tracking

Any large ship or facility maintains its own supply track, separate from the character's personal supply track.

**The character away from the ship:** The two tracks are fully decoupled. The ship's supply does not change from the character's activity on the ground.

**The character aboard the ship:** When the character returns for a genuine stay (long enough to sleep, eat, and operate from the ship across at least a session beat or two), their personal supply resets to the ship's current value. The ship's track is then the active track for all purposes.

**The character departing the ship:** Their personal supply is set to the ship's current value at the moment of departure.

**Resupply from the ship while away:** The character's personal supply cannot exceed the ship's current value. Resupply from other sources (scavenging, barter, raider stores, settler resources) is uncapped.


## Resisting Suffer Moves
When a suffer move lists a "Resist:" option, you may make a resist roll after applying the main result. If the meter is at 0, this is mandatory. If two stats are listed, roll the higher.

Each suffer move specifies which stats or meters to use. Roll and compare as a normal action roll, results are listsed at the move.
